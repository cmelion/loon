# Request for information: stratospheric flight service for a connectivity pilot

*Draft for the author to send, 2026-10-17. Two addressees, Aerostar (Thunderhead) and World View (Stratollite), the same text to both. `results/deployable_now.md` names these answers as the first numbers the programme needs. Nothing in this draft commits anyone to anything; it asks for a configuration-specific specification, an interface document and a budgetary price.*

## Cover

We are preparing a civil-protection connectivity pilot in Europe: a Loon-class LTE/NR payload flown on a navigable stratospheric balloon, one Mediterranean summer campaign in 2027 and one northern winter campaign in 2027–28 at 55–62°N, three to six flights of two to six weeks each, recovered by parachute or parafoil to pre-cleared drop zones. We are asking for the information below so that a budget and a host-state application can be written against your vehicle rather than against public brochures. Budgetary figures are enough; we will come back with a formal request for quotation once a host state is named.

## A. Vehicle and configuration

1. Which configuration of your vehicle would you propose for a 2–6-week winter flight at 55–62°N, and which for a Mediterranean summer flight? Please give, per configuration, the dated specification sheet.
2. Customer payload mass, after your own navigation, power, telemetry, flight-termination and recovery hardware are counted: the mass that is ours.
3. Payload electrical power: continuous daytime, continuous night-time, and peak, at 55°N in January and at 40°N in July. State the battery energy allocated to the payload and what happens to payload power when the bus is short.
4. Operating altitude band, and the altitude range the navigation system uses at those latitudes and seasons.
5. Thermal environment at the payload interface, and any radiative-cooling or heating allowance.
6. Payload volume, mounting, field of view for downward-looking antennas of about 1 m, pointing or stabilisation offered, and the limits on centre of mass.
7. Data interfaces to the payload and the telemetry bandwidth available to us through your link.

## B. Flight performance at our latitudes

8. Flights you have flown north of 50°N in December–February: how many, how long, and what altitude control and station-keeping or slow-drift performance they achieved. A summary table with dates and latitudes is enough.
9. The dwell you would expect over a 500 km region at 50–55°N in January and in July, with the forecast product you navigate on, stated as a distribution rather than a best case.
10. Loss rate per flight to date (termination not at the planned point, payload not recovered), and the recovery accuracy you would plan for with a parachute and with a guided parafoil.

## C. Operations

11. Launch sites you can operate from in Europe, or the requirements for a site we provide: pad size, hangar, surface-wind limits, gas supply (helium or hydrogen), crew size and lead time.
12. Launch rate you can sustain from one site, per day and per week, in winter weather.
13. Who holds the airspace authorisation and the flight-termination decision in flight, and how you handle a border approach.
14. Export control: whether the vehicle, the flight computer or the navigation software carry ITAR or EAR restrictions that affect a European civil customer, and whether flight data can be owned by the host state.
15. Insurance you carry, and what third-party liability cover you would expect the customer to provide.

## D. Price, budgetary

16. Price per flight for a four-week flight at the configuration in A, including launch, flight operations, termination and recovery of the payload, as a range.
17. Mobilisation and campaign costs for a six-week window at a European site, separate from the per-flight price.
18. Payload integration and qualification support, as a day rate or a package.
19. Price of a lost vehicle, and the cost of a replacement flight after a weather scrub.
20. Earliest campaign dates you could offer for summer 2027 and winter 2027–28, and the notice you need.

## E. Upgrade path

21. Whether a configuration with 90–100 kg of customer payload and 1–1.5 kW of continuous daytime payload power exists or is planned, and on what date.
22. Whether a hydrogen-lifted variant exists and whether a payload fuel cell drawing from the lift gas has been considered or flown.
23. Whether you would license or partner on a European-built vehicle, and under what conditions.

## What we will do with the answers

Replace the placeholder figures in the pilot budget, write the mass, power, thermal and link budget against your interface document, and take the host-state application forward. We would welcome a call once you have had a look, and we are happy to sign a non-disclosure agreement for the configuration-specific material.
