# Mitigation research: what to do about each weakness

*2026-10-17. Four research briefs, one per cluster of `results/weakness_register.md`, each answered independently by the four models best suited to it (winds and vehicle: gpt-5.6-sol, gpt-6.1-sol, grok-4.6, gemini; radio and security: gpt-5.6-sol, grok-4.6, gpt-6-sol, gemini; operations and airspace: gpt-5.6-sol, gpt-6.1-sol, gemini, grok-4.6; economics: gpt-5.6-sol, gpt-6.1-sol, grok-4.6, plus kimi-k3 if it returns). Sixteen answers, about 250 KB, read in full and adjudicated here. Precedents the reviewers leaned on were spot-checked against the public record the same day (Aerostar, HiDRON, Sceye, WRC-23). Verdicts: MITIGATE (fix it), DESIGN-AROUND (change the architecture so it stops mattering), ACCEPT (live with it and say so), AVOID (drop the feature). Costs and durations are the reviewers' planning allowances, not quotations.*

## The four decisions this forces

The reviewers converged, across clusters and without seeing each other, on four changes to the architecture. Everything else is a repair.

1. **Winter is a transit product.** Nobody could cite multi-week altitude-steered station-keeping at 45–55°N in December–February. Loon's record is tropical, NASA's Wānaka flights were autumn drift, and the February 2023 balloon that crossed North America is evidence of the conveyor, not against it. The one lead worth chasing is Aerostar: its Thunderhead flew 336 days from Florida through a full winter (April 2024 to March 2025) with station-seeking stretches of 17 days at 100 % on station, using an altitude band up to 28 km and machine-learned wind models. The latitudes and months of those holds are not public. Ask Aerostar for January keep-in-box tracks at 45–55°N before accepting the verdict; until then, design winter as a one-way conveyor with degraded service and say so.
2. **The radio is Loon-class until proven otherwise.** 2 Gbps per balloon survives in no answer. One beam of 20–40 MHz at 2–4 bit/s/Hz is 40–160 Mbps; a metre-class 2.6 GHz array with generous spectrum might reach a few hundred Mbps; under WRC-23 power-flux limits and real operator holdings, tens to low hundreds of Mbps per platform is the defensible planning value. Every capacity, outage, per-gigabyte and Starlink-match figure in the repository moves by 10–50× when this is applied. The product becomes prioritised voice, text and thin data for responders and the public, which is also the only product the spectrum and power budgets support.
3. **The radio comes home under a parachute, not on a glider.** The airspace cluster was unanimous: around 800 autonomous 40 kg gliders a day descending through European controlled airspace at 15–35 m/s is a certified-category UAS programme larger than the balloon programme, not a recovery subsystem, and U-space does not cover it. The repository's own glider work stands as research, but the v1 recovery architecture is a guided parachute or parafoil into a small number of pre-cleared drop zones (what Loon, NASA and World View actually do), with a lighter recoverable module or an expendable radio for surge. This reopens the economics in `TECHNICAL_ISSUES.md` §1, since the forecast-release penalty was computed for a glider.
4. **The product is exercised readiness, and it has a competitor to beat.** A thin standing layer plus surge is the right shape, but its cost is a desk estimate and its stock cannot field the surges it quotes. More importantly, one reviewer priced the obvious alternative for a narrowly defined 30-day, 300 km, one-million-person event (satellite terminals at critical sites and public hubs, transportable cells, a direct-to-cell messaging retainer): roughly €8–21 M a year of readiness and €6–14 M per activation, against €165 M per activation for the balloon voice-and-text case. Those are transparent allowances, not quotes, and the service is narrower than phone-direct coverage. But it is the number the balloon layer has to beat, and Ukraine since 2022 is the empirical version of it.

## Winds, vehicle and power

**W1 Winter westerlies.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). No precedent for winter station-keeping at this latitude; ships move the entry point, not the conveyor, and in winter the exit is east over land, so Atlantic launch shortens European dwell. Mobile western land launch (Loon's autolauncher) is the only operational lever. Validation: ERA5 0.25° directional-shear histograms, 45–55°N, 30–150 hPa, December–February, ten held-out years, then re-fly the controller on those fields (free to €30 k, two to six weeks). Open lead: Aerostar's winter tracks, as above. Residual: cost stays three to four times summer unless winter service is dropped or degraded.

**W2 One year, coarse grid.** MITIGATE (4/4). Ingest ERA5 at 0.25° on all pressure levels, run 2010–2025 with 2025 held out, remove the 70°N clamp; use archived GFS or ECMWF forecasts (TIGGE) for every decision the controller makes, because reanalysis is hindsight. €5–70 k, three to ten weeks. The qualitative winter finding survives; every number changes.

**W3 Unflown light vehicle.** MITIGATE (4/4). Build the vehicle model first (volume, free lift, pump flow against pressure, vertical rate, thermal inertia, film creep, leakage), re-fly the drift and recovery sets with it, and only then fly one to three instrumented 7–14-day flights with a 40 kg mass simulator. Model €20–100 k, one to three months; flights €0.15–4 M, nine to fifteen months. Until this is done every dwell and recovery figure belongs to the Loon-class vehicle, and the 40 kg budget must be shown to close with the glider, battery, array, pump and optical terminals inside it.

**W4 Thin film at 1,000 Pa.** MITIGATE, and AVOID it as the cost baseline (4/4). Carry the proven heavier film in the cost case; run a coupon campaign at 1,000 Pa, −80 °C, UV and ozone, fold-crease and a thousand diurnal cycles (€0.3–2 M, six to twelve months), then subscale and full-scale flights. One reviewer: the quietest fact here is that the main cost lever and the main reliability risk are the same object. A tendon-supported lobed envelope is the structural alternative to asking the film to carry the load.

**W5 Winter power.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). 300–600 W through a 16-hour night is 4.8–9.6 kWh; a pack for that is 50–70 kg and does not fit a 40 kg payload. Specify a reduced night service, keep lift gas out of the fuel cell, and rerun `analyze_power.py` with the light vehicle's mass, array area and 50°N January irradiance rather than BLE's solar model. Chamber-test the complete power train through ten winter cycles before any night-service claim.

**W6 Loops and the Arctic.** AVOID (4/4). Hard termination at a latitude and FIR boundary; delete hemispheric loops, the Canada loop and "transatlantic" from the operational case and the prose. Rerun the hemispheric set with ERA5 to the pole only to size border-crossing probabilities.

**W7 Release timing costs dwell.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). Place recovery sites on the real winter exit corridors (Atlantic façade in, eastern land out) and rerun `forecast_release.py` with the causal rules; price expendable radios as the alternative. Ships shadowing the conveyor and airborne catch systems (SkyHook, Gremlins) are not credible at fleet scale and belong out of the baseline. Note that decision 3 above changes this analysis: a parachute to a drop zone has a different reachable set from a glider.

**W8 Glider design and attrition.** Superseded in part by decision 3. For the research track: adopt the held-out winner (L/D 18 at 45–60 kg/m²) only with a terminal parachute, cost the airframe, actuators and avionics, and run ten development and fifty qualification drops. Accept attrition well above the 2 % the model assumes, and report reaching the area, landing safely, retrieval and serviceable reuse as four separate probabilities.

**W9 Return without GNSS.** AVOID in v1 (3/4), MITIGATE with GNSS plus inertial and a large landing ellipse. Daylight star cameras exist on balloon-borne telescopes but give attitude, not position; unaided inertial drift over two hours is kilometres; vision fails over cloud, sea and snow. Keep a bounded safe-termination mode and stop claiming precision recovery under denial.

**W10 Descent winds.** MITIGATE (4/4). Port `jet_test.py` to ERA5 0.25° full column across ten held-out years, add a turbulence model for the terminal phase, and drive release and descent from the same archived forecast. €5–30 k, two to five weeks. Expect the headline to stay near the site-network ceiling of 98.3 %; expect the terminal phase to be the open question.

## Radio, network and security

**W11 2 Gbps per balloon.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). Rebase to 0.05–0.5 Gbps per platform (Loon delivered 10–40 Mbps; HAPSMobile and Sceye have published calls and video, not cell capacity) and rerun `outage_scenario.py` at 0.04, 0.2 and 0.5 Gbps. Write the beam, spectrum, power-flux and power workbook against WRC-23 masks and one operator's 10–20 MHz. Hardware test: an SDR base station with a 0.5–1.6 m array on a stratospheric flight, throughput to phones on the ground, €1–4 M, nine to eighteen months.

**W12 Aperture.** MITIGATE (4/4). Two layers: a 700–900 MHz umbrella with 40–80 km cells for coverage and uplink, and 1.8–2.6 GHz spots of 5–15 km from a 1–1.6 m array for capacity. Delete the 1.7 km spot and the 5 m low-band array from the article. Antenna model and a tower or tethered range test, under €50 k.

**W13 Ordinary handsets.** MITIGATE (3/4). Unmodified LTE phones at this geometry are demonstrated in principle: Loon, HAPSMobile and both direct-to-cell operators adapt the network side (timing advance, cell size) rather than waiting for Release 17 handsets, and Doppler at 70 km/h is trivial. The open items are the 14° edge uplink, indoor penetration in a winter blackout (15–25 dB, and every demonstration so far was outdoor), handover between cells moving at 10–70 km/h, and a certified handset matrix. One winter radio flight at 50°N with a commercial base station, €2–8 M.

**W14 Spectrum and borders.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). WRC-23 identified the bands under Resolutions 213 (694–960 MHz), 221 (2 GHz) and 218 (2.5 GHz) with power-flux limits and "no more interference than a terrestrial base station" conditions; it granted no rights, and no EU emergency instrument for a third-party airborne base station exists or is drafted. The workable form is disaster-only, hosted on an operator's licence, under national temporary authorisation, with transmitters muted at borders and operating co-channel only where the terrestrial network is down. A standing Union-wide HIBS service is years of CEPT and Commission work. Legal instrument map €50–300 k; coexistence simulation against a live 700/800 MHz network €50–100 k.

**W15 Backhaul.** MITIGATE (4/4). Recut the mesh at 500–700 km, run a time-resolved connectivity simulation over the actual tracks with gateway locations, acquisition outages and weather-correlated gateway loss, and price gateways and allied satellite overflow as infrastructure. Routing churn across drifting nodes degrades TCP; Loon's Tempest work is the precedent. Simulation €30–150 k; a two-node optical flight test €1–5 M and not before the radio rate exists.

**W16 Island mode.** MITIGATE (4/4). This is procurement: a deployable 5G SA core (3GPP IOPS from Release 13; FirstNet and Vodafone Instant Network as operating precedents), Release 17 disaster roaming with host operators, pre-provisioned responder identities, 112 routing and location with each national PSAP, lawful-intercept policy, and timing holdover without GNSS. Avoid a green-field European PLMN. Bench and tabletop €0.15–0.5 M; a multistate exercise €1 M and up.

**W17 Jamming.** ACCEPT the reach argument, AVOID the decibel-margin claim (4/4). The 25× smaller visibility area is a real reach advantage against distant uplink interference and nothing more; a jammer in the street wins against any altitude; the uplink into a balloon is the worse geometry; 443 nodes are 443 victims; ordinary phones cannot use the panel. Protected terminals for designated users are the one real mitigation and must be stated as not the mass market.

**W18 Denial of service and command plane.** MITIGATE (4/4). 3GPP access-class barring, MPS and mission-critical services, provisioned before the event in the MTPAS and WPS pattern; island ingress control, which is the one defence a global ISP cannot copy but which conflicts with 112, lawful intercept and satellite overflow and so is a policy decision; and command-and-control treated as aviation safety under DO-326A and NIST 800-193 with per-vehicle keys, offline revocation, out-of-band links and safe behaviour on loss of command. Load test €0.1–0.5 M; assurance programme €0.5–2 M over one to two years.

## Operations, airspace and environment

**W19 Daily descents.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). See decision 3. The route that might clear tens of descents a day this decade is state-aircraft status under Regulation 2018/1139 Article 2(3) with national civil-protection rules, pre-cleared rural or maritime drop zones, Mode S and a parachute; the route that clears 800 autonomous gliders a day does not exist. Pre-application study with EASA, Eurocontrol and one national authority €0.1–0.5 M; a hundred instrumented descents into a segregated offshore range €2–5 M. Annex 2's seven-day notice for heavy balloons is itself incompatible with surge and needs a standing arrangement.

**W20 Overflight and air defence.** MITIGATE in peacetime, AVOID the contested-airspace claim (4/4). Country-by-country authorisation under Annex 2 Appendix 4, forecast-based termination before borders with error margins (six hours of drift is 430–860 km), Mode S and NATO identification so the recognised air picture does not treat the envelope as a 2023-class unknown. At 20 km a polyethylene envelope is a slow target inside every modern air-defence envelope; the war case the series began with is the case the layer cannot serve, and the article should say so.

**W21 Envelope debris.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). No biodegradable film holds 1,000 Pa for weeks at −70 °C; bio-based polyethylene is still polyethylene; burning at altitude is a permitting killer. The options are to recover the envelope with the payload, to stop disposing of envelopes by moving to a reusable platform, or to accept a quantified loss rate and permit against it under MARPOL Annex V and the single-use plastics regime. Rerun the mass flow in `cost_comparison.md` at full land recovery, a third at sea, and zero recovery. The reviewers expect this, not cost, to decide public acceptance.

**W22 Hydrogen.** MITIGATE (4/4). Ordinary industrial practice: on-site electrolysis so inventory is hours, ATEX zoning, a written safety case, separated fill cells. 443 launches a day at 5–7 kg is about 3 t a day across the network, a 7 MW electrolyser class in total; a ship deck in a winter Biscay is the part with no precedent. HAZID and ATEX layout for one site €30–150 k.

**W23 Surge geometry and throughput.** DESIGN-AROUND (4/4). 48 launches a site a day is a model artefact; Loon, Aerostar, World View, CNES and NASA launch one to eight large balloons a day from a hangar, and the light vehicle still needs a 15 m-class inflation. At an honest 6–12 a day, 443 launches need 40–80 hangars and 1,476 need over 120, pre-permitted, around the compass, with correlated weather closure. Write `surge_dispatch.py` with weather-limited throughput, crew shifts and hydrogen supply over ERA5 held-out winters; run a 24-hour trial at one site aiming for 24 launches. Size the first service to what that shows.

**W24 Time to coverage.** ACCEPT as a multi-day product (4/4). The honest metric is time to 10, 50 and 90 % of required capacity, not first balloon; a standing layer helps only when a track happens to be upwind. Rerun: for every hour and every candidate zone, time to N balloons from a cold start and from 50 or 100 aloft, on the existing tracks and then ERA5. Sub-day grid failures are outside the product and the article should name Iberia as such.

**W25 Coverage as availability.** MITIGATE (4/4). Write `availability.py`: hourly occupancy of GHSL or GEOSTAT population cells by 40, 80 and 194 km discs on the real tracks, service ended at forecast release, capacity allocated across overlapping cells, population-weighted availability, outage-duration tails, winter separated, ten held-out ERA5 years. Replace the random-placement branch in `cost_model.py`. Expect winter tails to collapse the year-round figure; that is information.

## Economics, programme and evidence

**W26 Cost against Starlink.** DESIGN-AROUND (3/3). Stop leading with per-km² and per-gigabyte ratios; compare defined services (restoration deadline, concurrent users, delivered throughput, priority, duration, control dependencies) and report cost per protected site-day and per delivered emergency session. Correct the 10,000× to the roughly 5,900× the table gives. Keep the ratios in the appendix; they will be quoted anyway.

**W27 Readiness cost.** MITIGATE (3/3). The €0.2–0.5 B figure is withdrawn until a discrete-event inventory and staffing model exists. The reviewers' arithmetic: at 443 launches a day a 500-radio stock gives 1.1 days per circulation, which is impossible; a three-to-seven-day circulation needs 1,300–3,100 payloads (€33–76 M of radios alone); the network needs 30–50 permitted sites, not 10–15; and a thin standing layer exercises operations at 25–50 launches a day, which does not demonstrate 443. Standing-plus-surge goes into the cost model as one scenario ledger.

**W28 Funding and the development bill.** MITIGATE (3/3). Named bases: rescEU under Decision 1313/2013/EU as amended by 2021/836 and Implementing Decision 2019/570, which needs a new capacity category; the EDF (2021/697) for collaborative defence R&D only; IRIS² (2023/588) and GOVSATCOM (2021/696) as the dedicated-programme precedents; FirstNet's 2012 statute and 2017 contract as the responder-network precedent. The credible first route is national civil-protection and defence procurement for a gated pilot. Development allowances before any fleet: €0.3–0.75 B over four to six years for the envelope, vehicle, radio and recovery system (gpt-6.1), or €2.5–5.5 B over seven to ten years including industrialisation and a multi-country pilot (gpt-5.6); limited operational capability 2032–2036 from a 2027 start. Verify the IRIS² figures against the regulation and concession, not a September 2026 recap.

**W29 The counterfactual.** MITIGATE (3/3), and this is the hole. Write the service specification first (population, area, time to service, 72-hour and 30-day cases, concurrent users, rates, 112, priority, island operation, control requirements), then issue one common request for quotation to OneWeb distributors, governmental satcom suppliers, deployable-cell integrators, operators and direct-to-cell providers, with two lots: messaging plus critical sites, and ordinary-phone regional data. The illustrative should-cost above (€8–21 M a year plus €6–14 M per event) is the bar for the narrow lot. Write two counterfactuals, one under non-US control and one without that constraint; they are different products. €40–100 k and eight to twelve weeks for the comparison.

**W30 Why this, why now.** DESIGN-AROUND (3/3). Responders and multi-day regional failure are the market; the 4–8 M permanently uncovered are not, and dividing €6.3 B by them gives €790–1,575 per person a year. Report three denominators separately: payers, potential beneficiaries inside the authorised territory, and delivered beneficiaries. Write `results/why_now.md` as a written delta from Loon's 2021 closure: envelope cost, radio, recovery, and the LEO and direct-to-cell baseline that did not exist in 2018. Ukraine's and NATO's commercial LEO spend is the revealed willingness to pay the layer must beat.

**W31 Evidence quality.** MITIGATE (3/3). Public archive with container, data manifest, seeds and one-command tables; frozen choices; at least three untouched winters; decisions from archived forecasts; an unaffiliated reproduction of the headline tables. €20–200 k.

**W32 Capability creep.** AVOID (3/3). Connectivity-only baseline with a payload and data charter; any sensing or electronic-support variant is a separate programme with its own customer, permits and governance. Do not invoke ITAR generically; EU dual-use rules and GDPR are the applicable frames.

**W33 Framing.** MITIGATE (3/3). An options appraisal in the Green Book and Better Regulation form, with the service specification, the counterfactual, the gates and a cancellation rule in the first page, and a claim-to-source ledger marking each statement demonstrated, modelled, estimated or aspirational.

## Weaknesses the panels added

Appended to `results/weakness_register.md` as W34–W50. In one line each: launch-weather availability and common-mode grounding of upwind sites by the storm that caused the outage; pump, valve and compressor cycle life and authority on the light craft; thermal control, descent condensation and glider icing; a configuration-controlled mass and volume budget, since the thin envelope, long-range glider and winter power cannot all be assumed inside 40 kg; fleet common-mode behaviour (identical controllers on one forecast cluster or terminate together); interference from hundreds of airborne cells into surviving terrestrial networks and the moving-cell radio-network problem (physical-cell identities, neighbour lists, handover storms); indoor uplink in a winter blackout; TDD synchronisation and fleet timing under GNSS loss; 112 location and caller identity from an 80 km cell; handsets that have exhausted their batteries searching for a network before the balloons arrive; en-route air-traffic and 1090 MHz occupancy from hundreds of transponding balloons; third-party liability and insurance under Regulation 785/2004, probably requiring a state indemnity; hangar consenting as a five-to-ten-year programme; maritime recovery logistics for a third of landings; emergency airspace competition with rescue and firefighting aircraft; environmental permitting beyond litter mass (habitats, batteries, hydrogen leakage); procurement, state-aid and concession structure; supplier and control-chain sovereignty component by component; stranded assets and stop gates; simultaneous emergencies across regions; subscriber trust and key custody in island mode.

## The reruns, in the order they pay

All in this repository, all cheap relative to any flight, each one changes a published number.

1. ERA5 0.25° ingest (`fetch.py` replacement), 2010–2025, full column, no 70°N clamp. Everything below depends on it.
2. December–February directional-shear histograms at 45–55°N, 30–150 hPa, and an upper bound on dwell with a perfect controller (W1).
3. `jet_test.py` and `forecast_release.py` on ERA5 with archived forecasts for the decisions and recovery sites re-placed on the exit corridors (W7, W10).
4. The light-vehicle model and re-fly of the drift set (W3), with `analyze_power.py` on 50°N January irradiance (W5).
5. `outage_scenario.py` at 0.04, 0.2 and 0.5 Gbps per balloon, and at 6–12 launches a site a day (W11, W23).
6. `availability.py` on population cells (W25), and the time-to-N-balloons surge replay from cold start and from 50 or 100 aloft (W24).
7. Mesh connectivity on the tracks at 500 and 700 km with gateway sites (W15).
8. FIR and border tagging of every track (W20), and the envelope mass flow at three recovery rates (W21).
9. One scenario ledger that ties launches, inventory, mass, capacity and cost together for standing-plus-surge (W27).

## Where this note differs from the reviewers

- On W1 the reviewers wrote Aerostar off as marketing. The 336-day flight through a winter and the published station-seeking statistics are real; what is missing is the latitude and month of each hold. That is a question to ask, not a precedent to dismiss.
- On W13 one reviewer said handsets are undemonstrated; the better view, which two others took, is that outdoor camping is demonstrated by Loon and both direct-to-cell operators, and the gaps are indoor, edge and handover.
- On W17 one reviewer called jamming a trap to be avoided entirely; the reach advantage is real and is kept, as a reach claim.
- On W32 one reviewer again preferred disclosure to removal; the baseline stays connectivity-only, with any sensing variant disclosed as its own programme.
- The two development bills differ by an order of magnitude because one stops at qualified subsystems and the other runs to industrialisation. Both are kept; the article should quote the range and the reason.
