# Reruns on the M4, 17 October 2026

Results from the machine that runs the engine, for the M3 and the productions.
Each section gives the command, the number before, the number after, and what
it changes. Small outputs are committed in `results/`; wind data are release
assets (`data-era5`, `data-ncep`), not tracked files.

## 1. ERA5 ingest gate (January 2025)

The fetch was split into one request per month (CDS refused a year of 15
levels in one request: "cost limits exceeded") and run with `--keep-nc`, so the
raw monthly netCDF files are kept until the comparison is signed off.

- **NaN fraction:** 0.000000 for u and v, in both the Europe and the band files.
- **Single point (50°N 10°E):** January mean u rises monotonically to 30 hPa
  with no 250 hPa peak, in ERA5 and NCEP alike (within ~1 m/s at every level).
  Not a data fault: the January 2025 jet core sat far south of 50°N.
- **Box mean, 30–60°N, 0–20°E** (`profile_check.py`, the runbook's criterion):

| hPa | ERA5 | NCEP | GFS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 300 | 15.8 | 16.2 | – |
| 250 | 17.9 | 18.3 | – |
| 200 | 19.5 | 20.0 | – |
| 150 | 20.1 | 20.4 | 19.1 |
| 100 | 19.0 | 19.3 | 18.7 |
| 70 | 18.1 | 18.4 | 18.5 |
| 50 | 18.8 | 19.1 | 19.7 |
| 30 | 23.6 | 23.9 | 24.9 |

  The tropospheric jet core is 41.1 m/s at 200 hPa at the southern edge of the
  box (30°N; the single-point check found it at 27°N along 10°E). The polar-night
  jet is at 60°N above 70 hPa. ERA5 matches GFS within 1–1.3 m/s at all five
  overlapping levels. **Gate passed.**
- **Two fixes to `profile_check.py`:** its "latitude of max" was offset by the
  box's first row (it printed 25°N, outside the box, for ERA5 and NCEP), and its
  "peak" line picked the 30 hPa polar-night jet; a tropospheric-jet line
  (500–100 hPa) was added.

## 3. Winter power budgets (`winter_power.py`, no engine, 50°N, 60 hPa)

- **Loon-class tie-back (BLE's array, 184 W night):** the array harvests 7.5–12.4 kWh
  a day all year, but November–January nights need 2.6–2.8 kWh, more than BLE's
  3 kWh battery at 80% depth: the mechanism behind the 1.8-day winter flights.
- **Light vehicle, 300 W night:** a December night needs 4.5 kWh, a 37.5 kg battery
  at 150 Wh/kg, which leaves −0.5 kg of the 40 kg payload. A larger array does not
  help; the 15-hour night does. Summer needs 16.8 kg.
- **Class M heavy vehicle (3 kWp, 1,000 W day / 300 W night):** January harvest
  31 kWh, surplus 21.7 kWh against a 4.4 kWh night, so the day closes; the 20 kg
  battery does not carry the whole night, which is the fuel cell's job.

## 4. Outage at Loon-class radio capacity (`BALLOON_GBPS=0.04,0.2,0.5 outage_scenario.py`)

300 km winter voice-and-text zone: **2,545 balloons and 4,429 launches a day at
0.2 Gbps**, against 254 balloons and 443 a day at 2 Gbps; about 370–740 launch
sites at 6–12 launches a site a day. Regional outage backfill only works with
gigabit-class radios. (Per-flight cost in that script is still the earlier
€12,400 mid case.)

## 5. Class M heavy vehicle, drift (`VEHICLE=heavy run_drift.py`)

Without a fuel cell (the drift script has none), class M loads end most flights
out of power: mean dwell 2.35 days against 5.39 for BLE's balloon (Little's-law
weighted 1.15 against 3.65); 86 of 120 slow-controller flights end OUT_OF_POWER.
May–July average 5–7 days but still lose 20–40% of flights to power. The array
patch reaches BLE's solar model; the likely cause is BLE switching to night
loads whenever solar output is below the 1,000 W day load, so mornings and
evenings drain the battery. Treat this run as "class M without a fuel cell", not
as the vehicle's dwell. Output: `results/drift_2025_heavy.csv`.

## 6. Class M heavy vehicle, winter on the lift gas (phase 3 gate)

`VEHICLE=heavy WINTER=1 MAX_DAYS=60 FUEL_CELL=vent run_multipass.py`, 20
November–February launches from the five sites:

- Survives **6–11 nights (median 8.5)**; median flight 9.2 days.
- 19 of 20 end ZEROPRESSURE after burning **10–17% of the lift gas** (median
  1.58 kg of hydrogen); one ends NO_SAFE_ALTITUDE.
- January launches: 5.9–9.9 days.

**Gate reading:** for one-way winter service it passes, because a January
crossing lasts only 1.3–1.9 days and the fuel cell carries class M loads for
about a week. For winter loiter or multi-pass loops it fails at one to
one-and-a-half weeks; going further needs hydrogen that is not the lift gas.
Output: `results/multipass_2025_nochina_fcvent_winter60d_heavy.jsonl`.

## 7. Nordic launch set (`LAUNCH_SET=nordic run_drift.py`), first run

| | Nordic (Copenhagen, Gothenburg, Riga, Tallinn) | Original five sites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean dwell (slow) | 3.28 d (Little 2.15) | 5.39 d (3.65) |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | 1.91 d | 2.55 d |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | 4.26 d | 9.92 d |

The winter expectation holds; the summer one does not: Nordic sites are less
than half as good as the western and central sites in summer. Their best months
are spring (Gothenburg 12.3 days in April, Copenhagen 6.4–8.3 days April–June)
and September (Riga 6.4, Tallinn 4.8). Tallinn is weak all year (1.85 days).
By site: Copenhagen 4.03, Gothenburg 3.95, Riga 3.31, Tallinn 1.85 days.
Output: `results/drift_2025_nordic.csv`.

**Dwell is the wrong measure for these sites; northern coverage is the right
one.** Hourly tracks (`LAUNCH_SET=nordic track_slow.py`, 96 flights; the output
tag now carries the launch set so the baseline `slow_tracks.csv` is never
overwritten) against the 120 baseline tracks, over a northern box 54–59.5°N,
5–30°E (Denmark, southern Sweden, the Baltic states, the Baltic Sea):

| | Existing 5 sites | Nordic 4 sites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours over the northern box per flight | 8.6 | 26.2 |
| Share of region time spent in the north | 7% | 33% |
| Months with no northern coverage | January–March, October | none |
| Cell-hours of coverage per flight (0.5°×1° cells, 80 km radius) | 49 | 152 |
| Weakest quarter of northern cells (76 cells) | 296 cell-hours | 3,555 cell-hours |

The existing sites pass within 80 km of 92% of northern cells at some point,
but rarely and never in winter or early spring. **A network that serves the
Baltic and Scandinavia needs the Nordic launch set alongside the western and
central sites;** it is a coverage requirement, not a dwell optimisation.
Outputs: `results/slow_tracks_nordic.csv`, `_summary.csv`.

LAUNCH_SET=canada MAX_DAYS=60 BATTERY_WH=10000 THRUST_MS=3`): median 5.6 d over
Canada, 4.2 d Atlantic, 11.6 d Europe; useful share 46% (Europe-only 26%),
every flight reached Europe, 69% clipped China. Same result as NCEP (46%,

70°N clamp hid how far north flights go. Output:
`results/multipass_2025_nochina_bat10000_thrust3_canada_era5.jsonl`.
