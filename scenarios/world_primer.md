# World primer — the Kessler-world balloon network

One page on what is real, what is extrapolated, and the rules the simulations impose on the story. Every number below is traceable: repo results in `results/`, episode files in `scenarios/ep*/facts.md`, outside facts cited inline.

## What is real today (cited)

- **Low orbit is crowding.** ESA's Space Environment Report 2025 (data to end-2024): in some heavily used altitude bands the density of active satellites is now the same order of magnitude as debris; fragmentations add debris faster than it re-enters, and intact objects re-enter more than three times a day ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2025), [UN-SPIDER summary](https://un-spider.org/news-and-events/news/esa-space-environment-report-2025)). The story's "Kessler world" moves this from warning to fact.
- **The launch market is concentrated.** SpaceX flew 165 of 325 orbital launches in 2025; Ariane 6 flew about four ([results/cost_comparison.md](../results/cost_comparison.md), sources listed there). Balloons need no launch vehicle; that is their one structural advantage.
- **Iberian blackout, 28 April 2025.** Grid restoration took 6–10 hours; mobile calls failed on some networks; Vodafone Spain ran at about 70 % capacity on backup power ([AP](https://m.washingtontimes.com/topics/red-el/), [Riskline](https://riskline.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Advisory-Spain-Portugal-Blackout.pdf), [bebeez.eu](https://bebeez.eu/2025/05/03/how-did-iberian-operators-fare-in-the-great-power-cut/)).
- **GNSS jamming around Kaliningrad and the Baltic.** EASA named the area in 2022; Lithuania's regulator says Kaliningrad jammers reach beyond 400 km and more than 30 sources operated in 2025 ([gpsworld.com](https://www.gpsworld.com/european-agency-warns-of-gnss-outages-near-ukraine/), [lrt.lt](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2862230/gps-jamming-incidents-reported-by-pilots-continue-in-lithuania-regulator-says), [Spire](https://spire.com/blog/space-reconnaissance/gnss-interference-report-russia/)).
- **2025 fire seasons.** EU: worst EFFIS year on record, ≈1.03–1.08 Mha; Spain ≈401,000 ha, mostly in August in León, Zamora and Ourense ([tovima.com](https://www.tovima.com/climate/eu-records-historic-wildfire-losses-in-2025/), [aa.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/greenline/climate-change/europe-faced-record-breaking-wildfires-in-2025-report/1829004), [euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/08/19/worst-wildfire-season-in-decades-burns-over-340000-hectares-in-spain)). Canada: ≈8.9 Mha, second worst on record ([NRCan situation report](https://cwfis.cfs.nrcan.gc.ca/report?embed=true), [Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/severe/2025-is-canadas-second-worst-wildfire-season-on-record-for-area-burned)).
- **The balloon itself is Loon's.** The simulator is Google's Balloon Learning Environment: 1,804 m³ superpressure envelope, 68.5 kg envelope, 92.5 kg payload, 3.06 kWh battery, 184 W night load, altitude control by pumping air ([BLE `balloon.py`](../.venv/lib/python3.10/site-packages/balloon_learning_environment/env/balloon/balloon.py); Loon was shut down in January 2021, see cost_comparison.md sources). The winds are real 2025 winds: GFS hourly over Europe (`data/gfs_europe_2025.npz`) and NCEP/NCAR 6-hourly around the hemisphere.

## What is extrapolated

- **The world.** A near future in which low-orbit services are unreliable or distrusted, Russia and China deny overflight, and an allied (EU, UK, Norway, Türkiye, Canada) stratospheric network is the connectivity backbone. No date is given; "a few years after 2025".
- **The hardware.** Today's BLE balloon is flown as-is in every episode. The tuned radio glider (L/D 14, 20 kg/m², crab guidance plus speed-to-fly) and the €1.1k thin-film envelope exist as designs in `results/glider_tuning.md` and `results/envelope_cost.md`, not as flown hardware. Fuel-cell stores (10 kWh) and 3 m/s propellers exist only as options in `run_multipass.py`.
- **Radio capacity.** 1–5 Gbps per balloon in the outage model is an assumption for a modern phased-array payload; Loon measured ~10–40 Mbps shared.
- **Costs.** Every euro figure is a Monte Carlo median with a P10–P90 range built from tagged assumptions (`cost_model.py`, `combined_cost.py`, `envelope_cost.py`).
- **Place names.** Tracks are 2.5°-grid wind physics with BLE noise; "over Vienna" means the track point is within a few tens of kilometres.

## In-world rules (each derived from a simulation result)

1. **Winter flights last about two days; summer flights a fortnight.** Monthly mean dwell over the region: Jan 2.4, Feb 2.1, Mar 1.5, Apr 6.1, May 6.5, Jun 14.0, Jul 9.0, Aug 6.8, Sep 6.7, Oct 3.3, Nov 3.3, Dec 3.1 days (slow controller, `results/drift_2025.csv`). Half of winter flights are under 48 h. Fastest of 120: 14 h (Episode 1).
2. **Balloons cannot hold station over Europe.** Station-keeping gave ~4 % of time within 50 km of a target (`run_coverage.py`); the network is a conveyor of drifting balloons, and launch rate scales with 1/dwell (Little's law). 95 % year-round coverage needs ~1,100 launches/day (1,871 in winter, 453 in summer).
3. **Launch rate is the budget.** ~€11k per flight with the redesigned envelope, tuned glider and gig pickup (€6–25k); fleet ~€3.4 B/yr ≈ 12× Starlink per km² (`combined_cost.py`). The base case with today's hardware is €38.7k/flight and €15.5 B/yr.
4. **Radios glide home 98 % of the time.** With the tuned glider and the "last hour with ≥100 km margin" release rule; 78 % with today's glider and rule (`results/glider_tuning.md`). The ceiling is 98.3 %: two flights a year never pass within glide range of any site.
5. **Overflight of Russia and China is forbidden; 42 °E is the eastern wall.** The simulation's region ends at 42 °E, 47 % of flights exit there (56 of 120), and every long-life flight crosses Russia; with corridor logic and thrust 88 % still clip China (`run_multipass.py`). Rule: terminate at the last recovery opportunity before the wall (Trabzon, Sochi is off-limits in-world, Constanta, Odesa, Kyiv).
6. **Envelopes come down on land two times out of three.** 66 % land, 13 % Black Sea, 5–16 % Mediterranean, 13 % Atlantic; parachute drift median 26 km (`analyze_envelopes.py`). Cut down at the radio release, not at the region edge, to keep the Mediterranean share low. 27 kt/yr of polyethylene at base-case scale.
7. **A balloon that goes north in winter dies.** Beyond ~60 °N in November there is no sun; a 10 kWh store lasts ~2.5 days of night load and the power-floor logic freezes altitude control (Episode 7, `multipass_2025_nochina_bat10000_thrust3.jsonl`: every November launch died of power).
8. **Ships don't beat land sites, but they can be anywhere.** Atlantic ship launches match the best land sites (arrivals ~5.4 d on NCEP winds vs 4.6 d for land) and 13 % never arrive; choosing the best launch site per date is worth ×1.6–1.75 dwell with hindsight (`run_ship.py`).
9. **Western Canada launches are the best use of a long-life balloon.** 46 % useful time (Canada + Europe) versus 26 % for Europe-only launches; all reach Europe, typically on day ~10 (`LAUNCH_SET=canada`).
10. **Hydrogen is free, film is cheap, parts and launches are not.** H₂ is 0.3 % of cost; the residual envelope cost is fittings, seams, labour and QA (`results/envelope_cost.md`); payload and envelope are 78 % of the base-case spend.
11. **A blackout backfill is a surge of launches, not a parked fleet.** Iberia (58 M people, April winds): voice/text needs ~435 balloons aloft and 172 launches/day at €2.1 M/day; degraded data 1,450 aloft, 573/day, €7.1 M/day; first coverage 14–35 h after launch (`scenarios/tools/outage_case.py`).
12. **No GNSS, no landing.** A 5 m/s unknown wind over a 2 h glide is a 37 km error against a 25 km recovery circle; the 100 km arrival margin is what makes a local beacon approach possible (Episode 4).
